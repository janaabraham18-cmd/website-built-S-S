One confident statement in the display serif, large — not three photo rows easing you in. Beside it, a vitals stub styled like the inside cover of a passport: the numbers that matter, read in two seconds.

This is the front page of the dossier, not a corporate "About Us" — it tells you who's driving before it shows you the scenery.

02

The centerpiece

The Route

New — the page's signature moment

Mockup — interactive route map
Sossusvlei Where the dunes turn red at dawn

A stylized map of the two basecamps with routes fanning out to Sossusvlei, Etosha, Skeleton Coast, Sandwich Harbour. Hover a route — it lights up and a preview card surfaces: photo, one line, "See this tour."

This reuses the idea behind the site's best existing feature — the map on the tours page that assembles itself as you scroll — and gives it the homepage stage it deserves instead of leaving it one page deep. This is the "boutique concierge" moment: fun, interactive, and unmistakably this brand.

Each tour becomes a boarding-pass card — a torn ticket stub carrying the duration, a name set in the display serif, and a one-line hook. A light filter bar (Desert / Coast / Culture / Combo) makes browsing 31 options feel curated, not overwhelming.

The existing alternating photo/copy rows still live underneath each ticket when expanded — this is a skin and an entry point, not a rebuild of the content.

04

Proof, not polish

The Logbook

Replaces the flat Gallery grid

Mockup — scrapbook masonry
— early light, Sossusvlei
— low tide, Pelican Point

A loose, mixed-size masonry instead of a uniform grid, with a few photos carrying a short handwritten-style caption — like pages torn from a guide's own logbook, not a stock photo wall.

Uneven and specific reads as real; perfectly uniform reads as stock. This is the section where imperfection is the point.

Why us, briefly

The same numbers from The Brief's vitals stub, now blown up into circular, ink-stamped marks with a rough dashed edge — like a passport stamped at each border crossing.

It rhymes visually with the Itinerary's ticket stubs a few stops back, so the "travel document" language holds together instead of feeling like a one-off gimmick.

06

Guest voices

Postcards from the Road

Replaces the star-rating carousel

Mockup — fanned postcard stack

"Our guide knew the dunes like his own backyard."

— J. Marsh, Sossusvlei Tour

"Dolphins right next to the boat, no hard sell."

— R. Okafor, Catamaran Cruise

Reviews as a loosely fanned stack of postcards — a stamp corner, a handwritten-feeling quote, a name and the trip taken — rather than a plain star-rated carousel that could belong to any business.

Click or swipe fans a new card to the front. Small, tactile, and it keeps the dossier's physical-object language going right through the social proof.

07

The quiet one

Before You Go

Replaces the FAQ preview grid

Mockup — plain editorial accordion
Do I need to book in advance?+
Where do tours depart from?+
Can tours be combined?+

After six stops of personality, this one is deliberately calm: a plain accordion, serif question, sans answer, no stamps or tickets. Luxury also means knowing when to stop performing.

It's the section a nervous first-time booker actually needs to be boring and clear — this is where trust gets closed, not where it gets sold.

08

The invitation

Plan Your Route

Same closing CTA, reframed copy

Mockup — closing CTA
Ready to plan your route?

Visually unchanged from today's closing photo card — it already works. Only the heading changes, from "Plan your journey" to "Plan your route", closing the loop the hero opened.

Nine stops, one sentence each could summarize: arrival, who's driving, where you could go, what you'd actually do, proof it's real, why trust it, who's said so, the practical questions answered — then the invitation.
